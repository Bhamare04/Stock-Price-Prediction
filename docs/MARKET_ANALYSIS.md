# AI Market Analysis

The prediction response combines the existing LSTM next-day estimate with the latest technical indicators and the model's persisted holdout errors. It is an analytical explanation, not a trading recommendation or a guarantee.

## Prediction interval

For a ticker with saved evaluation output, the service calculates residuals in actual stock-price units:

`error = actual_price - predicted_price`

The lower and upper residual percentiles (5th and 95th) are added to the new predicted price. This produces the `prediction_range` returned by `POST /predict`. The interval is unavailable when no historical actual-versus-predicted data exists, rather than being invented.

## Confidence category

Confidence describes relative interval width; it is not the probability that the prediction will be correct. The interval width is divided by the current price:

`relative_uncertainty = (upper - lower) / current_price`

The current configurable thresholds are:

- `Low`: below 3%
- `Moderate`: 3% through below 7%
- `High`: 7% or greater

These thresholds are in `services/market_analysis_service.py` and should be treated as an interpretable heuristic.

## Technical signals

The service reuses the existing RSI, SMA, and EMA calculations and adds MACD and its signal line to the indicator output. RSI is labeled oversold, overbought, or neutral. MACD is positive or negative based on its relationship to the signal line. The moving-average signal compares the current price with SMA 20, falling back to EMA 20 when needed.

The final explanation is generated by deterministic rules from the predicted direction, signal agreement or conflict, and interval-based confidence. It does not call a paid external AI service and does not use BUY or SELL language.

## Limitations

The LSTM is trained on historical closing prices and cannot account for news, corporate events, liquidity changes, or future regime shifts. Yahoo Finance data can be delayed or unavailable. A narrow historical error interval does not guarantee a narrow future error, and a confidence category is not calibrated statistical probability. All outputs remain estimates and are not financial advice.
